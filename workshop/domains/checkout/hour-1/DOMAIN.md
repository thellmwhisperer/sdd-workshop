# Product: Shop checkout

## The client's brief

We run three small clothing shops and our tills are old. We want a checkout page on a
tablet at the counter: the cashier adds products to a basket, sees the total and takes
payment. Some products carry a discount sticker. Customers often bring back part of what
they bought, and today that is a mess of paper notes.

## The story

Claire and Julien Morel own Fil, three small clothing shops in Lyon with six cashiers.
Their tills are twelve years old and the vendor stopped supporting them last spring. In
the Christmas sales one till died, and the shop in the Croix-Rousse wrote every sale on
paper for two days. A normal Saturday afternoon: eight people in the queue, a shirt with a
30 % sticker that the cashier works out on a calculator, and a customer who brings back
one of the two shirts she bought last week. The cashier writes the refund on a paper note.
At closing time the notes and the drawer do not match, and Claire spends Sunday morning
finding out why.

## What we know about the business

- Last month: 41 returns across the three shops, and 6 of them were refunded with the
  wrong amount.
- 78 % of sales are paid by card. On Saturdays, at the shop next to the market, almost a
  third are cash.
- Prices on the shelf include VAT: in France a shop must show the customer the final
  price.
- Élodie, their accountant, files VAT every quarter and needs the VAT total of every sale.
- A discount sticker comes off the price before VAT, never after. Élodie checks it in
  every quarter's books.
- 1 return in 5 comes without a receipt. Last year two of those were for clothes bought in
  another shop.

## The goal

A checkout on a tablet at every counter before the January sales. Done, for Claire, means
a cashier closes a basket with a sticker and a partial return in under a minute, and the
drawer matches the screen at closing time.

## Open decisions

The group decides each one with the chief. The chief writes the decision below it.

### 1. Payment methods

Card only, or card and cash? Cash means counting a drawer at closing time; card only is
simpler, but some customers only carry cash.

What we know: Card is 78 % of sales, but the market shop takes almost a third in cash on
Saturdays.

**Decision:** Card and cash.

### 2. VAT on screen

Prices with VAT included, or VAT as a separate line? Included is what customers see on the
shelf; separate is what the accountant wants.

What we know: The law asks for the final price on the shelf; Élodie needs the VAT total of
each sale.

**Decision:** Prices include VAT; the receipt shows the VAT total at the end.

### 3. Returns without the receipt

Allowed or not? Allowing it is friendlier; refusing it avoids refunds for things that were
never bought here.

What we know: 1 return in 5 has no receipt, and two of those last year were for clothes
bought elsewhere.

**Decision:** Only with the receipt.
