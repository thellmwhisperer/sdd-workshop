# Product: Shop promotions

## The client's brief

Our marketing team runs promotions every week: second unit at half price, 20 % off
jackets, buy 3 pay 2. Today the cashier has to remember them and apply them by hand, and
gets them wrong. We want promotions defined once and applied at the till.

## The story

Jorge Sanz leads marketing at Brisa, nine sportswear shops around Madrid. Every Monday his
team of two decides the week's promotions and emails a PDF to every shop. The cashiers
print it, pin it next to the till, and apply each promotion by hand. On a busy Saturday a
cashier forgets that the second pair of socks is half price, the customer notices at home,
and on Monday there is a complaint and a refund. Last week a jacket got 20 % off twice,
because it was in two promotions at once.

## What we know about the business

- An audit of one month found 1 promoted basket in 8 charged wrong: about 3,100 euros,
  some given away and some overcharged.
- Promotions change every Monday. IT is one person, two days a week.
- Customers who see what they saved come back more often, says Jorge's last survey.
- Promotions never stack: a product gets one promotion, the best one for the customer. The
  jacket at 20 % twice broke that rule.
- The receipt printer prints on narrow paper; every extra line makes the queue a little
  slower.

## The goal

Promotions defined once and applied at every till before Black Friday, the busiest week of
the year. Done, for Jorge, means no cashier has to remember a promotion, and the Monday
complaints stop.

## Open decisions

The group decides each one with the chief. The chief writes the decision below it.

### 1. Who creates promotions

Marketing on a page, or a file that IT updates? A page gives marketing control; a file is
quicker to build and harder to break.

What we know: Promotions change every Monday, and IT is one person two days a week.

**Decision:** Marketing on a page.

### 2. When a promotion applies

Automatically at the till, or the cashier picks it? Automatic never forgets; picking lets
the cashier explain it to the customer.

What we know: 1 promoted basket in 8 was charged wrong when the cashier applied it by
hand.

**Decision:** Automatically.

### 3. Savings on the receipt

Show what the customer saved, or not? Showing it sells the promotion; hiding it keeps the
receipt short.

What we know: Customers who see what they saved come back more often; the paper is narrow.

**Decision:** Show the savings.
